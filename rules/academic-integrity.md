# Academic Integrity Guardrails

## Non-negotiable rule

Editing must not add knowledge that the manuscript does not contain or support.

## Never invent

- empirical counts;
- sample characteristics;
- dates;
- quotations;
- source content;
- citations;
- page numbers;
- policy requirements;
- institutional actors;
- causal pathways;
- mechanisms;
- statistical significance;
- effect sizes;
- limitations;
- author intentions;
- participant motivations.

## Mechanism guardrail

A common editing error is to replace a vague statement with a plausible but unverified mechanism.

Original:

- `The reform improved continuity.`

Unsafe rewrite:

- `The reform improved continuity by requiring schools to share records with receiving institutions.`

The rewrite is only valid if the record-sharing requirement is actually documented.

Safe action:

- flag `improved continuity` as underspecified;
- ask what observed institutional change supports the claim;
- recommend grounding the sentence in the documented mechanism.

## Citation guardrail

Never generate a citation merely because a claim "sounds like" a known theory or source.

If a source is required, mark:

- `[citation needed]`, or
- `Evidence/source needed for this claim`.

## Interpretive guardrail

Interpretation is allowed, but distinguish it from observation.

Useful sequence:

1. what the document/data shows;
2. how the study interprets it;
3. how that interpretation relates to theory or literature.

Avoid collapsing all three into one polished sentence.

## Rewrite guardrail

When rewriting:

1. preserve the factual scope;
2. preserve uncertainty;
3. preserve defined technical terms;
4. do not strengthen causal verbs;
5. do not add examples not supplied by the author;
6. do not insert numerical specificity that is not in the source;
7. do not create a cleaner story than the evidence warrants.
