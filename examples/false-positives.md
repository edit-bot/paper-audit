# False Positives to Avoid

PaperAudit is intentionally conservative. These are common cases where a simplistic "AI-style" checker can damage academic prose.

## 1. Standard reporting phrases

Do not flag merely for being common:

- `This study examines...`
- `The findings suggest...`
- `Previous research has shown...`
- `The analysis indicates...`

Flag only when repeated excessively or followed by vague content.

## 2. Passive voice

Acceptable:

> Documents were included if they established a formal pathway for recognition.

Passive voice can keep the methodological criterion in focus.

## 3. Inanimate subjects

Acceptable:

> The regulation requires schools to record the basis for attendance recognition.

The regulation genuinely encodes the requirement; replacing it with a human actor may reduce precision.

## 4. Technical repetition

Acceptable:

> Entry responsibility was coded separately from continuity responsibility. Continuity responsibility was then compared across pathways.

Repeating a defined construct is often preferable to stylistic synonym variation.

## 5. Three-item empirical classifications

Acceptable:

> Pathways were classified as transfer, retention, or support-only.

A genuine three-category coding scheme should not be rewritten merely because triads can also be a rhetorical pattern.

## 6. Short conceptual propositions

Potentially acceptable:

> Recognition is not equivalent to provision.

Keep if this is a defined distinction supported and used throughout the analysis. Flag only if it appears as a decorative one-line pivot.

## 7. Dense sentences

A long sentence is not automatically poor prose. If qualifications are necessary to preserve legal, institutional, or methodological precision, shortening can create error.

## 8. Discipline-specific convention

A predictable phrase may be the most transparent phrase. Do not force novelty into standard academic reporting.
